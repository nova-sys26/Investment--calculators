What is a Risk Reward Calculator?

A Risk Reward Calculator helps traders measure whether a trade is worth taking by comparing how much they could lose versus how much they could gain. It is one of the most useful tools in trading because good trading is not only about finding a good entry — it is also about knowing whether the upside is worth the downside.

Before entering a trade, you need to understand the amount at risk if the trade fails and the amount you can make if it succeeds. This calculator turns that into a simple comparison so you can make clearer decisions.

How This Calculator Works

The calculator uses your entry price, position size, stop loss, and take profit to estimate the dollar amount at risk and the dollar amount you could earn. It then compares those values to produce your risk/reward ratio.

Risk = |Entry − Stop Loss| × Position Size Reward = |Take Profit − Entry| × Position Size Risk / Reward Ratio = Reward ÷ Risk
  • Entry Price = the price where the trade begins
  • Position Size = number of units or shares
  • Stop Loss = the price where you exit at a loss
  • Take Profit = the price where you exit at a gain

The ratio is only part of the story. The distance between your stop and target also matters because it shows whether the trade structure is realistic.

Why Risk Reward Matters

A trade with a poor risk/reward profile can still fail even if the entry is good. A trade with a strong profile gives your strategy room to work and makes it easier to build positive expectancy over time.

Risk/reward is not about forcing every trade to be huge. It is about making sure the upside is large enough relative to the downside.

What the Results Mean

A ratio below 1 means the potential loss is larger than the potential gain. A ratio around 2 or more is often more comfortable for many trading styles, although the right number depends on win rate and execution quality.

This calculator also estimates the dollar amount you can lose and the dollar amount you can gain so you can judge the setup in practical terms, not just in abstract ratio form.

The Right Mindset

The right way to use a risk/reward calculator is to evaluate the setup before taking the trade. A high ratio alone does not guarantee success, but poor structure often makes success harder.

Even a trade with a great ratio can be a bad trade if the target is unrealistic or the setup is poorly structured.
